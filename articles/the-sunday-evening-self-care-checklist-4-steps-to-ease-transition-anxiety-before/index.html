If you get a sinking feeling in your stomach around 4:00 PM on Sunday, you aren’t imagining things. That shift from weekend freedom back to Monday morning alarms triggers a very real form of transition anxiety. For years, I used to spend my Sunday evenings scrolling my phone on the couch, feeling increasingly tense as the clock ticked toward bedtime, only to wake up exhausted and irritable on Monday.

Waiting out the anxiety doesn’t work. What finally helped me was treating Sunday evening not as the end of my weekend, but as a deliberate bridge into the new week.

Instead of vague advice to “relax,” I built a repeatable four-step checklist that takes about 45 minutes total. Here is the exact routine I use to quiet my mind and reclaim my Sunday nights.

Step 1: Do a 5-Minute Brain Dump

The biggest driver of Sunday night dread is usually the mental load of the upcoming week. When all your tasks, emails, and errands are floating around in your head, your brain treats them as urgent threats that you need to solve right now.

Grab a physical notebook—not your phone, which invites distraction—and spend five minutes writing down every single thing rattling around in your brain. Do not organize it or make a neat to-do list. Just dump it.

  • Write down the presentation you need to prep for Tuesday.
  • Note that you need to buy milk tomorrow.
  • Jot down the awkward conversation you’re dreading having with a colleague.

Once these thoughts are physically on paper, your nervous system registers that you won’t forget them. They are safely stored outside your head. Close the notebook and set it aside until Monday morning.

Step 2: Remove One Monday Morning Friction Point

Anxiety thrives on the unknown and the cumbersome. If you wake up on Monday to a cascade of small annoyances, your baseline stress level shoots through the roof before you even leave the house.

Choose just one physical or logistical hurdle and eliminate it right now. Do not try to prep your entire week; pick a single friction point that usually derails your Monday morning.

For me, it’s breakfast and coffee. On Sunday evening, I set up the coffee maker with fresh grounds and water so all I have to do is push a button. Sometimes, if I know Monday is going to be brutal, I will lay out my clothes or pack my gym bag ahead of time.

Pick the single task that usually makes you groan on Monday morning and do it in advance. It takes five minutes, but it buys you an immense amount of mental peace when you wake up.

Step 3: Shift Your Physical Environment

When we are anxious, we tend to stay frozen in place—often staring at a screen or pacing around the house. To signal to your body that the weekend is over and it is safe to wind down, you need a physical reset.

Step away from your phone and change your environment to engage your senses. I call this the “closing shift” for my living space:

  • Dim the overhead lights and turn on a single lamp.
  • Take out the kitchen trash so you don’t wake up to yesterday’s coffee grounds.
  • Change into your most comfortable, softest loungewear.
  • Brew a cup of decaffeinated tea or pour a glass of ice water.

This physical transition acts as a boundary marker. It tells your brain that work and chaos are locked outside, and the rest of the evening is dedicated entirely to comfort and recovery.

Step 4: Engage in a Low-Stakes, Analog Activity

The final trap of Sunday anxiety is trying to force yourself to go to sleep too early because you are worried about being tired. Lying in bed staring at the ceiling while your mind races only makes the anxiety worse.

Instead, spend 20 minutes doing something completely analog that requires zero performance or productivity. It shouldn’t be a chore, but it shouldn’t be doom-scrolling on social media either, which keeps your brain in a state of high stimulation.

Read a physical fiction book, do a crossword puzzle, stretch on the floor while listening to an instrumental playlist, or take a warm shower. The goal is to find an activity that absorbs just enough of your attention to keep you from catastrophizing about the workweek, while gently lulling your nervous system toward sleep.

Transitioning from the freedom of the weekend back to the structure of the workweek is rarely seamless, but you don’t have to suffer through it. By taking a proactive approach to clearing your mind and prepping your environment, you can turn Sunday night from a source of dread into a reliable anchor for your week.
